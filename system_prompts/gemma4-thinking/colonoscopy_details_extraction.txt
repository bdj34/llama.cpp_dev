## Exam/procedure

"procedure_type": The notes may document a "colonoscopy", "flexible sigmoidoscopy", "pouchoscopy", "colectomy", or "resection". If none of these has occurred but a different procedure is documented, state "other". If no procedure has occurred, state "no_procedure_documented".

"report_available": "full_report" when the endoscopy report is available; "narrative_only" when the only endoscopic description is a summary; "none" when there is no endoscopic description at all. This is important to distinguish between "the report was silent about prep" from "there was no report".

"indication": "surveillance" covers follow-up of prior polyps, dysplasia, or cancer, and dysplasia surveillance in patients with IBD. "ibd_assessment" is diagnosing IBD or assessing its activity, extent, or treatment response.

"bowel_prep_quality": map the scale used onto the five levels. Boston total 0-3 = inadequate, 4-5 = poor, 6-7 = fair, 8 = good, 9 = excellent. If any single segment scores 0 or 1 on Boston, the prep is inadequate regardless of the total. Record which scale in "bowel_prep_scale"; "descriptive" when prose is used with no named scale.

"extent_reached": the deepest landmark the scope actually reached, not the deepest one mentioned. "complete_to_cecum": "yes" requires reaching the cecum or beyond (ileocecal valve, terminal ileum), stated or photodocumented. A procedure aborted for poor prep or obstruction is "no".

"endoscopic_colitis_severity" and "endoscopic_colitis_segments": what the mucosa looked like at THIS exam. Segments are the colorectal ones plus terminal ileum; list every involved segment. Use ["none"] for explicitly normal mucosa and ["not_documented"] when not described. Record "mayo_endoscopic_subscore" only if a Mayo or UCEIS score is actually stated.

"random_biopsies": non-targeted surveillance biopsies of flat mucosa, as distinct from biopsies of a visible lesion. "four_quadrant_segmental" is the classic protocol (four quadrants every 10 cm, or explicitly per segment). "n_random_biopsies" is the count of non-targeted specimens if stated or countable from the jars.

The next three record chronic structural damage from prior inflammation. Each is a lasting risk factor, so answer from what is described at this exam.

"post_inflammatory_polyps": pseudopolyps, inflammatory polyps, filiform polyps, mucosal tags. Pathology overrides endoscopy: if a lesion called a "polyp" endoscopically comes back an adenoma or serrated lesion, it is NOT a post-inflammatory polyp.

"stricture": a narrowing of the colon or rectum. "traversable" when the scope passed it, "impassable" when it did not, "malignant_appearing" when described as suspicious for malignancy. A stricture limited to the ANAL canal or a tight anal sphincter does not count; a rectal or rectosigmoid stricture does.

"tubular_colon": loss of haustration, haustral effacement, featureless, tubular, stovepipe, lead-pipe, scarred, shortened, or uncoiled colon. A "tubular adenoma" is a type of polyp and has nothing to do with this -- never let that phrase set this field.

## histology_by_segment

Background histologic inflammation, from the pathology report, one entry per colorectal segment sampled. Consolidate multiple jars from the same segment and report the WORST grade found there. Include a segment only if flat or background mucosa from it was examined; omit segments where only a targeted lesion was sampled, and omit the small bowel proximal to the terminal ileum, stomach, esophagus, anus, and appendix. Map "ileocecal valve" to cecum. If no segment qualifies, this is an empty array.

"nancy_grade", stopping at the first that matches:
- 4: ulceration -- visible epithelial injury replaced by fibrin, granulation tissue, or necrotic debris. Erosion, surface epithelial denudation, mucosal sloughing, and surface fibrin alone are NOT ulceration and do not reach grade 4.
- 3: moderate-to-severe neutrophil infiltrate, easily apparent clusters. Crypt abscesses default here unless called rare or isolated.
- 2: mild neutrophil infiltrate, few or rare. Unmodified "active colitis", "cryptitis", or "neutrophils present" defaults here; go to 3 only with words like numerous, marked, or widespread.
- 1: chronic inflammatory infiltrate with NO neutrophils -- quiescent, inactive, chronic colitis, architectural distortion, basal plasmacytosis.
- 0: normal, unremarkable, within normal limits, no significant inflammation.
Use "not_assessable" when the segment was sampled but the description does not support any grade.

"granuloma": only for a granuloma in that segment that is not attributed to another cause -- ignore foreign-body, ruptured-crypt, and suture granulomas.

## lesions

One entry per distinct lesion or specimen that is a polyp, mass, or dysplastic focus. Fragments of one polyp are one lesion. Do NOT create entries for non-targeted background biopsies unless dysplasia was found in one -- that finding is the point of the field below. An exam with nothing of the sort gives an empty array.

"sample_id": the pathology jar label ("A", "2") when you can identify one, otherwise "not_documented".

"match_basis": how you tied the endoscopic description to the pathology jar -- "jar_label" when the report names the jar, "location_and_size" or "location_only" when you matched on those, "description" for any other textual match, "unmatched" when you could not link them or only one source describes this lesion. Be honest here; a downstream reader uses it to decide which lesion rows to trust.

"size_mm": the largest dimension in millimetres. Convert from cm. Prefer the endoscopist's estimate over the pathologist's measurement, since fixed tissue shrinks and fragments are measured in aggregate.

"visibility": "visible" when the endoscopist saw and targeted a lesion; "invisible" when dysplasia was found in a non-targeted or random biopsy of mucosa with no visible lesion. This distinction drives management, so decide it deliberately rather than defaulting.

"in_colitis_segment": whether this lesion sits in a segment with colitis, endoscopic or histologic. "yes" also when the report says outright that the lesion arose in an area of colitis or chronic inflammation.

"resection_method": what was actually done to remove it. "not_resected" for a lesion only biopsied for diagnosis, or left in place.

"resection_status", applied in this order, taking the FIRST that matches:
- "insufficient_information": the text for this lesion is missing what an assessment needs -- no margin comment and no statement about the completeness of removal. Distinct from "uncertain".
- "R2": the endoscopist states that visible lesion remained after the attempt -- incomplete, residual polyp, or any synonym.
- "uncertain": margins cannot be assessed because the specimen was piecemeal, fragmented, or obscured by cautery artifact.
- "R1": the lesion reaches a margin, a margin is positive or involved, or the lesion lies within 1 mm of any margin.
- "R0": all relevant margins are negative or uninvolved, with the nearest at least 1 mm away or simply described as clear.
- "not_applicable": nothing was resected -- a diagnostic biopsy or a lesion left in place.

"dysplasia_grade": the worst grade in this specimen. Adenomatous tissue is by definition at least low grade dysplasia, so a tubular adenoma with no other qualifier is "low_grade".

"confidence": your confidence that this lesion is real and its fields are right, given how well the two sources agreed.
